About Research CV

I am a PhD student in economics at The University of Texas at Austin. I work in labor economics, political economy, economic history, and the economics of education. My research studies the causes and consequences of economic inequality and mobility in education, housing, and political representation, including the role of discrimination and how these patterns change over the long run.

My current work examines how family origins shape entry into political office and legislators' policy choices, how marriage markets transmit elite status across generations, how college admissions testing policy affects who enrolls, and how school choice affects neighborhood change.

My advisors are Sandra Black, Peter Bergman, and Eric Chyn, and my research is supported by an NSF Graduate Research Fellowship. Before graduate school, I was a consultant at the Boston Consulting Group, a data analyst for Elizabeth Warren's presidential campaign, and a Fulbright English Teaching Assistant in South Korea. I received a BA in Applied Mathematics from Harvard College.
